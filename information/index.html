About Occupational Therapy

Understanding what OT is, who it can benefit, and how we put the right support in place for you.

The Basics Winnie Ly

What is Occupational Therapy?

Occupational therapy (OT) is a health profession that helps people of all ages participate in the activities — or occupations — that are meaningful to them. These include self-care, work, leisure, and social roles.

An occupational therapist works with you to identify any barriers — whether physical, cognitive, emotional, or environmental — that are preventing you from living life to the fullest. Together, we develop practical strategies and interventions to overcome those barriers.

The ultimate goal is to improve your ability to do the things that matter most to you, and to increase your independence, confidence, and wellbeing in everyday life.

OT helps with the full range of daily life:

  • Personal care & self-management
  • Domestic tasks & home management
  • Work, education, and volunteering
  • Leisure, hobbies, and social activities
  • Community participation & mobility
Who We Help

Who Can Benefit from OT?

Occupational therapy is for people of all ages and backgrounds. You may benefit if you are experiencing difficulties with everyday tasks due to any of the following:

Physical Health Conditions

Including stroke, acquired brain injury, musculoskeletal conditions, chronic pain, arthritis, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, and other neurological conditions.

Mental Health Conditions

Including anxiety, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, and other conditions affecting daily function and wellbeing.

Developmental Conditions

Supporting children and adults with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), ADHD, dyspraxia, learning disabilities, and developmental delays affecting daily life.

Older Adults

Helping older adults maintain independence and safety at home, prevent falls, manage age-related changes, and adapt to new challenges as they arise.

Injury, Surgery & Recovery

Supporting rehabilitation after fractures, orthopaedic surgery, or other injuries — helping you regain function, adapt techniques, and return to daily activities safely.

Long-Term Conditions

Living well with chronic or degenerative conditions such as cancer, heart disease, dementia, or long COVID — managing fatigue, adapting routines, and preserving quality of life.

Not sure if OT is right for you? The above list is not exhaustive. If you are unsure whether occupational therapy could help, please get in touch — the initial consultation is free, with no obligation.
How We Work

Our Approach

Every individual is unique. Our approach is built around understanding you as a whole person, not just your condition or diagnosis.

Person-Centred

We begin by really listening — to your story, your goals, and what matters most to you. Your priorities drive everything we do, ensuring interventions are meaningful and motivating.

Evidence-Based

All assessment tools and therapeutic approaches are grounded in current research and professional best practice guidelines set by the RCOT and other leading bodies.

Holistic

We consider the interplay between you, your occupations, and your environment — whether physical, social, or cultural — to create truly comprehensive support.

Collaborative

We work alongside you, your family or carers, and other health professionals to coordinate care, share information, and achieve the best possible outcomes.

What to Expect

The OT Process

From your first contact through to achieving your goals — here is what a typical OT journey looks like.

1

Initial Consultation

A free 30-minute phone or video call to discuss your situation, what you are hoping to achieve, and whether OT is the right fit for your needs. No obligation.

Free of charge
2

Assessment

A comprehensive assessment of your functional abilities, daily routines, home environment, and goals. This may take place at your home, another preferred location, or via video.

3

Treatment Plan

Based on the assessment findings, we develop a personalised plan with clear goals, agreed interventions, and a timeline to review progress at regular intervals.

4

Ongoing Support

Regular follow-up sessions to implement the plan, build skills, monitor progress, and adjust goals as needed — until you have achieved the outcomes you set out to reach.
